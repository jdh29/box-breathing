The Drill

Four equal counts, four sides of a square. Sit or stand upright, feet flat, one hand on your belly. Breathe all the way out before round one.

01 Left side - Breathe inIn through the nose for four counts. The hand on your belly moves first, your chest second. Fill smoothly rather than gulping.
02 Top - Hold fullFour counts with the air in. Keep your throat open and your shoulders down. You are pausing, not straining.
03 Right side - Breathe outOut through the nose or pursed lips for four counts, slow and even. Aim to still have a little air left at the end of the count.
04 Bottom - Hold emptyFour counts with the lungs quiet. This is the hard side. Stay relaxed through it and go straight into the next round.

Why It Works

Slowing to roughly four breaths a minute, with a pause at each end, shifts you out of a fight-or-flight pattern. Heart rate settles, the shaking in your hands eases, and the counting gives your attention one simple job instead of whatever spun you up.

Military and emergency crews use this one - some call it tactical or combat breathing - because it needs no equipment, works standing in the dark, and nobody watching can tell you are doing it.

When To Run It

  • Before: the interview, the heavy set, the conversation you have been putting off.
  • During a spike: when your hands go unsteady and your thinking narrows to a point.
  • After: to come down, so leftover adrenaline does not keep you wired for an hour.
  • To sleep: run it lying down and let the counts stretch out.

Progression

Four rounds is a reset. Eight to twelve is a session. If four counts feels like a strain, drop to three and stay there for a week - a pace you can hold calmly beats a longer one you have to fight. Only lengthen the count once the current one feels boring.

Before You Start

Ease off if you feel light-headed, tingly, or short of breath: shorten the holds, or just breathe normally for a minute. Skip the breath holds and talk to a doctor first if you are pregnant, or have a heart or lung condition, high blood pressure, or a history of fainting or seizures. Never practise this while driving or in water.
